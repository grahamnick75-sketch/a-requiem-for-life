How words sound

a = ah · e = eh · i = ee · o = oh · u = oo · ae = ay. sh and th are each a single sound. Stress often falls on the next-to-last syllable.

A simple sentence

Mi ti i tal mer. — I remember your name.
Ti mi i tal mer. — You remember my name.
Saelun nu vel. — The bond does not vanish.

Short clauses usually follow subject → object → verb. The question particle ke goes at the end; nu means not.

The Kethil alphabet

Pronunciation
a = ah; e = eh; i = ee; o = oh; u = oo; ae = ay. “sh” and “th” are single sounds. Stress normally falls on the second-to-last syllable. No letters are silent.

How to read a simple sentence
Ordinary order: who acts, then who or what is affected, then the action. A possessed thing follows its owner: mi i tal = my name. The word ke at the end asks a question. The word nu before an action means not.

A note on counting
In surviving tally texts: lin = one, dus = two, vos = three, kel = four, thal = five. Larger numbers combine these words, such as thal-lin = six or dus-thal = ten.
Kethil: surviving letter forms
Each sign represents one sound. “ae,” “sh,” and “th” each have their own signs. The Conservatory uses spaces and modern punctuation in teaching copies; the oldest inscriptions are less regular.

An example

Mi ti i tal mer.   =   I remember your name.
Field note: scholars sometimes disagree about poetry, but the basic syntax of many short sentences is well understood. Different languages and magical traditions coexist in the Fog; the First Tongue is one historical tradition, not the origin of all magic.
