1. Origin and survival
The First Tongue was spoken among supernatural inhabitants of the peninsula long before the
settlement that became San Francisco. Its speakers lived in the same landscape as the Yelamu, but the
language, its written tradition, and the fictional Fog communities are wholly invented. It is not an
Ohlone language and does not stand in for Indigenous history. The Currents belong to the human
connections and experiences of this place; Fog speakers named and studied them, but did not create
the human events that make them resonate.

The language faded as supernatural communities dispersed, intermarried, and adopted more widely
used tongues. No single catastrophe, historical human community, or supernatural conspiracy is
established as its cause. Today no ordinary Fog community speaks it fluently. Individual ancient beings
may remember isolated phrases, but even they are not guaranteed full knowledge.

Its literary remnants survive as inscribed stones, carved thresholds, annotated artifacts, damaged
scrolls, and copied glosses. The Conservatory maintains a small table of linguists and paleographers
within its existing library under the Conservatory of Flowers. They compare repeated formulae, trace
word families, and publish provisional readings for other Scholars.

3. The sound of the First Tongue
Lyrical but weathered: open vowels, resonant consonants, and occasional hard edges. The aesthetic
evokes fog, tide, headlands and wind; it is not borrowed from real Indigenous phonologies. Most words
are short enough to speak naturally at the table.

Stress Usually the penultimate (next-to-last) syllable. Eshara = eh-SHAH-rah; Orith = OR-ith; Saelun =
SAY-loon; Mereth = MEH-reth; Vaedrin = VAY-drin. Compounds preserve original roots but follow
normal word stress.

Spelling The written alphabet assigns one sign each to ae, sh and th. Most roots concatenate directly.
A hyphen in a Scholar’s romanization marks a vowel boundary that might otherwise be mistaken for
ae; esha-en uses three vowel sounds where appropriate. Hyphens are editorial, not extra sounds.

Rhythm No silent letters. Final consonants are gentle rather than heavily released. A pause often falls
before an important name or at the end of a clause.


4. Grammar that actually works
Simple clause structure
The ordinary order is SUBJECT – OBJECT – VERB (SOV). Time, place and other modifying phrases sit
before the verb. There are no gendered pronouns, and the same root may function as a noun or verb
depending on position; context and particles disambiguate. A present-tense “to be” is usually omitted.

The particles ya and vei specify a completed event and an intended future event, not exact tense.
Neither is required if time is clear. The simplest future example is “Mi ti vei mer.” (I will remember
you). A conditional can be written with sel (“if”) and ava (“then”): “Sel ti mi mer, ava sin ner.” (“If you
remember me, then we remain.”)

Arc 3 lullaby: temporal and manner expressions
Sen begins a subordinate time clause: sen mi vae = when I move on/pass on. No sen mi vae = and when
I move on. Hara is a quality/manner word placed immediately before the imperative marker and verb.
Birdie’s four-line chorus, in reconstructed First Tongue:
 Mi ti vei mer.           I will remember you.
 Ti mi vei mer.           You will remember me.
 No sen mi vae.          And when I move on.
 Mi i meren hara ha lun. Hold my memory gently.
The older Gilded Gull inscription Mi ti mer. (I remember you) preserves the same words without
vei (future intention). Each line can be engraved on an independent tablet for Arc 3.

Adjectives, compounds and derivation
Describing words follow what they describe: var nar = a person alone; tal nor = a forgotten name.
Compound words usually combine a first element that qualifies or directs the second: sae + lun = a
mutual holding; mer + eth = remembering that endures. Compound interpretations can become
idiomatic over centuries.

Counting in fives
The recovered accounting vocabulary uses groups of five. A larger unit comes first, with smaller
remainders afterward; hyphens in romanization mark joined tally words, not extra sounds. This is a
linguistic convention of the fictional Fog population, not a claim about historical Yelamu counting
practices. Short inscriptions most commonly record quantities below twenty-five.

6. Kethil: the engraved writing system
Kethil (keth “engraved mark/write” + il “line/stroke”) is the name Conservatory Scholars give the
recovered alphabetic script. The name is a translated compositional label, not a claim that every
ancient speaker called it this. Inscriptions run left to right; short gaps divide words, and later copyists
introduced punctuation. Most stones lack modern-style capitalization.

Each sign corresponds to one sound. The vowel ae and consonants sh and th have single dedicated
signs. There are no hidden letters, silent vowels, or arbitrary substitutions; a player with this chart can
accurately transliterate any word that uses the standard alphabet.




Pronunciation and glyph shape are independent. The alphabet was designed as an original fictional
geometric “tide and stone” script, not adapted from Indigenous imagery or historical Ohlone writing.

Orthographic conventions
A stone-cutter leaves a half-glyph width between words. Dots at clause boundaries are not Kethil
letters. When two phrases are paired, the second may mirror the first in syntax or word order; that
feature becomes important in the Alcatraz puzzle. Older inscriptions may join compound names
directly (Saelun), while a grammatical exercise separates their components (sae lun).

Research & puzzle design

THE FIRST TONGUE / CONSERVATORY LANGUAGE ARCHIVE




How translations become adventure clues
Early in the campaign, a Conservatory Scholar can teach the signs for mi, ti, mer and tal, or give the
party a partial chart. Later, inscriptions reveal familiar roots paired with new particles. The players
need never memorize a real dictionary; physical handouts and supportive NPCs make deduction
possible. Comprehend Languages, rituals, a historian’s contact or a clever nonlinguistic bypass should
help rather than stall the adventure.

What it does NOT do First Tongue is not automatically a command language that overrides magic.
Speaking Saelun does not manufacture connection. Its value is in recording the ancient supernatural
community’s observations of forces created through human experience.


8. Three playable language puzzles
Puzzle A — The Name That Returns
Recommended use Alcatraz, Arc 3, as an optional revelation or one path to understand how
remembrance resists Identity Fracture. Not a mandatory condition for redemption.

Place three distinct inscription fragments where the party can find them while investigating. Fragment
1: “Mi ti i tal mer.” Fragment 2: “Ti mi i tal mer.” Fragment 3: “Saelun nu vel.” Give the players only the
glyphs; let them use a previously found or Conservatory-provided partial key.




Decoding solution I remember your name. You remember my name. The bond does not vanish.

Narrative resolution A player recognizes that mutual recognition matters more than a literal magic
password. To weaken the haunting, a character can name another’s humanity, protect a stranger who
feels familiar, recall a shared act or choose to stay with someone even without memories. The player
never needs to recall a previous session verbatim.

Adventure puzzles

Fairness A good Insight check reveals that lost memories do not erase instinctive care. A Scholar can
reveal that mer = remember. If decoding stalls, a surviving echo of a genuine supportive act offers the
same insight. Do not lock Arc 3 behind perfect pronunciation.

Puzzle B — The Crossing Threshold
Recommended use A travel or identity-themed shrine, hidden door or threshold. Keep its placement
flexible; it need not become an established ancient site until introduced in play.

Two tablets show the independent signs “vae” and “drin”. A third stone bears the compound
“Vaedrin”. The party also sees two sketches: a road crossing water and a figure stepping into a new
silhouette. The question is which concept the full word names, not the precise spelling of an
incantation.




Solution Vae means cross; drin means choose to become. Together Vaedrin means passage into
chosen transformation. Opening the threshold may require a freely chosen action rather than physical
movement alone.

Alternate success If a player explains the idea in ordinary language, it works. If no one solves it, a
successful History, Investigation or relevant background check yields one of the roots.

Puzzle C — An Archive Misreading
Recommended use A low-stakes Conservatory research encounter that teaches the difference
between script and interpretation.

A damaged stone appears to say “Mereth saevar lu ner.” A hurried copyist assumes Saelun is named;
the party finds enough letters to show saevar is the ordinary compound “community,” not the Current
Saelun.




Solution “Memory endures within a community,” with Mereth poetically acting as the enduring force.
The phrase is not a call to awaken the Covenant Current, and the distinction matters to scholarship,
not because an NPC is deliberately lying.

Reward Tessa or Orun credits the party as co-translators and shares a useful contact or archival lead.
The Conservatory recognizes discovery without becoming the party’s employer.


9. Story hooks and future discoveries
The fivefold list Several independent inscriptions preserve the Current names in the same order.
Their fuller titles may have shifted over centuries, making “which version is oldest?” a viable research
mystery.

Continuity & quick reference

10. Continuity guardrails
1. The First Tongue is entirely fictional; avoid labeling vocabulary “authentic Ramaytush” or assigning
fabricated beliefs to Yelamu people. 2. Currents originated in this region and follow human
experience, but the supernatural inhabitants named them rather than caused human history. 3. Five
Currents: Eshara, Orith, Saelun, Mereth, Vaedrin; art and technological innovation are distinct. 4.
Vaedrin covers both cultural passage and chosen transformation without erasing Chinatown or the
Tenderloin. 5. The Conservatory researches and preserves the language; it does not govern the party. 6.
Arc 3’s entity can be fought or transformed through meaningful connection; one deciphered phrase is
not an automatic redemption button.

Scope of this release: a coherent game-usable First Tongue grammar, alphabet, lexicon and training
corpus. Dialects, metrical verse, complex mathematics, and formal magical terminology can be added
later without invalidating the foundation.
